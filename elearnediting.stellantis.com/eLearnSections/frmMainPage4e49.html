The air in the hydraulic circuit acts as a pad absorbing a large amount of the pressure exerted by the brake pump and reducing the efficiency of the braking. The air present is felt at the brake pedal making it seem "spongy" and reducing the braking capacity.

- Position the vehicle on a lift.

- Check that the brake/clutch fluid is between the MIN and MAX levels.

Use solely the specified brake-clutch fluid.

1. Remove the brake-clutch fluid reservoir cap and connect suitable pressure equipment for bleeding hydraulic systems to the actual reservoir.

To prevent impurities entering the brake-clutch fluid reservoir, before undoing the cap clean it and the entire upper surface of the reservoir next to the filler thoroughly.

To ensure the braking system is bled fully, the equipment service excess pressure must be adjusted to 1.0 bar.

- Remove the protective cap for the bleed valve on the brake calliper.

1. Connect the recovery device (pipe and reservoir) to the brake calliper bleed valve.

2. Open the bleed valve on the brake calliper.

- Slowly open the tap for the pressure equipment for bleeding hydraulic systems.

- Wait for all the air in the hydraulic system to emerge completely.

- Retighten the bleed valve on the brake calliper to the specified torque.

- Repeat the operations described above for the remaining brake callipers.

Whilst bleeding the air, always check that the level of the fluid in the pressure equipment for bleeding hydraulic system is correct.

The brake/clutch fluid collected should not be reused.

If the braking system is not fully operational after having carried out the bleeding operation, repeat the entire procedure after waiting a few hours for the brake-clutch fluid to settle down in the system.

- Remove the vehicle from the lift.
